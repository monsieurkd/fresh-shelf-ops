What this report covers

A fresh department loses money in two ways, and they are different problems. It gives margin away to sell stock before it expires, and it throws stock away because it bought more than it could sell. This report measures both over {{ trading_days }} trading days across {{ stores }} stores and {{ items }} items, then asks one question: given what is known about demand, how much should each store order, and when should it cut the price?

The answer is not to order less. Waste is a small fraction of sales and margin is a large one, so a plan that simply orders down destroys more margin than it saves in waste. The gains are in timing and in the specific lines whose cover pattern exceeds the life of the product. Those lines are named below.

Does markdown pay for itself?

A markdown buys time: margin spent to turn stock that would have been binned into revenue. Whether it pays depends on the line, the depth and the hour. The gap between what was printed and what the till honoured is priced separately, because it is a compliance problem rather than a pricing one.

Method and caveats

  • Recorded waste is a floor, not the loss. Disposals are scanned on a handheld, and on a busy evening about a fifth are never scanned. The size of that gap is measured, which is why the warehouse reconciles a stock ledger against physical counts instead of trusting the scan file.
  • Unrecorded shrink is a bound, not a measurement. It is the residue no record explains, and it absorbs unscanned loss, physical-count error and booking mistakes together. Count error dominates on this estate, which is why the headline figure is weighted by a confidence score.
  • The weather input is better than reality. The simulation gives the model perfect knowledge of future temperature. A real deployment has a forecast, and a forecast has error. This flatters the backtest and is the main reason to expect worse performance in production.
  • Markdown response is estimated from non-random history. Stores mark down the stock they expect to struggle, so the measured response understates the true one and the optimiser marks down slightly too little. Correcting that needs an experiment, not a better estimator.
  • The baseline is reconstructed, not observed. It models the order-up-to habit the stores run, rebuilt from delivery history. A real trial would use the actual orders placed.
  • This is a simulated estate. The sources, defects and economics are modelled. The pipeline, reconciliation and decision logic are not, and those are what the report demonstrates.
